01 • The Problem

Disasters don't wait for information to arrive.

During floods, earthquakes, cyclones, landslides and wildfires, information can become fragmented. Citizens, responders and authorities may need different information at the same time.

!
Fragmented information

Warnings, maps, field reports and resource information can exist in separate channels.

⌁
Delayed situational awareness

Decision-makers need a clear picture of affected zones, severity and changing conditions.

↗
Coordination gaps

Rescue teams, shelters, medical support and supplies need a common operational view.

⚠
Communication under pressure

Critical instructions should be easy to understand, locate and act upon.

THE ANSWERS RAKSHAAN PROVIDES

Instead of only saying “there is a disaster”, the prototype answers six practical questions.

01 — What is happening?Disaster type and current risk signal.
02 — Where is it happening?Affected-zone visualization and location context.
03 — How serious is it?Risk level, affected-area indicators and priority.
04 — Who needs help?People/areas requiring urgent attention.
05 — Who is responding?Rescue, medical and support-team status.
06 — What should happen next?Alert, evacuation, rescue and resource actions.

06 • Benefits

Designed around real response needs.

The goal is not another information screen. The goal is to make critical information easier to understand and connect the right response actions.

⚡ Faster information flow

A common interface can reduce the need to switch between disconnected information sources.

🗺️ Better situational awareness

Maps and visual risk layers help users understand where the problem is and how it is changing.

🤝 Better coordination

Teams, resources, shelters and medical support can be represented in one operational view.

📢 Clearer public communication

Alerts can focus on what happened, where it happened, severity and what action is recommended.

🌐 Scalable concept

The architecture can be extended to different disaster types, regions, languages and data sources.

🧩 Human-centered interface

Information is organized by decision needs rather than by technical complexity.
